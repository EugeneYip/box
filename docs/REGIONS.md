# World Map sizes, regional maps and real places — contract (round 13)

The World Map section of New World (title screen and the New World panel)
offers the whole Earth at three sizes plus six regional maps. Preloaded
realms and villages on every World Map world carry **real country and city
names**. Flags stay original: realms get the game's own banners and colours,
never real flags.

## 1. Choices under "World Map" (owner: Geography)

| Entry (shown as the size name) | id | Map | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large | `large` | whole Earth 512 × 256 | kept, not the default |
| Huge | `huge` | whole Earth 768 × 384 | kept, not the default |
| **Vast** | `vast` | whole Earth 1024 × 512 | **default on desktop** |
| Great East Asia | `great-east-asia` | 768 × 704, 73–150°E, 11.3°S–54.3°N, ~10 km tiles | East and Southeast Asia |
| Asia Mainland | `asia-mainland` | narrowed in round 14 to the East Asian mainland (section 4) | China, Mongolia, Korea |
| Japan | `japan` | 704 × 768, 128–146.5°E, 29.9–45.9°N, ~2.3 km tiles | the Japanese archipelago (south coast of Korea at the edge) |
| Taiwan | `taiwan` | portrait, widened in round 14 to include Kinmen and Matsu (section 4) | land under ROC rule only |
| Europe | `europe` | 832 × 640, 26.5°W–63.5°E, 32.2–73.8°N, ~7 km tiles | Iceland to the Urals, Scandinavia to the North African coast |
| North America | `north-america` | 832 × 640, 170–50°W, 6.2–75.8°N, ~12 km tiles | Alaska and Canada to Panama and the Caribbean |

The windows and sizes live in `src/world/data/regions.js` (shared by the game and the mask tool).

- "Vast-class": about the same tile count as Vast (1024 × 512 ≈ 524k tiles). Each regional map picks its own width and height so that, at the window's middle latitude, one tile spans about the same distance east–west as north–south (so Europe and Japan keep their real shapes). A tall region such as Taiwan gets a portrait map. Dimensions must suit the terrain chunking.
- Vast and the regional maps keep Vast's "desktop only" rule on touch devices. Touch devices keep Large as their default (a regional URL on a touch device falls back to it).
- URL: `?preset=earth&size=1024` for whole-Earth sizes; `?preset=earth&region=<id>` for a regional map (size is then implied: `params.size` is 1024 and `params.region` the id). New World and the title screen write these and reload as they do today; a change of region always reloads, even between maps of the same dimensions (Europe and North America). Loading labels say e.g. "Shaping Japan"; the top bar reads "Japan · Seed 7".
- The title screen shows the three whole-Earth sizes on one row and the six regions under "Regional maps"; New World does the same with cards drawn in each map's shape, and a note giving the region's tiles and tile size. On World Map worlds the "Starting villages" control reads "Auto (real places)" and shows the cap `src/sim/civ/realms.js` seeds with (26 on Vast and the regional maps).

## 2. Geography (owner: Geography — `src/world/earth.js`, `src/world/geo.js`, `src/world/data/*`, `tools/*`)

- `world.meta.geo = { region, lonMin, lonMax, latMin, latMax, wrap }` on every World Map world (`region: 'world'` for the whole Earth, `wrap: true` only there); `world.meta.region` repeats the id and `world.meta.kmPerTile` gives the tile size. `src/world/geo.js` exports `geoInfo(world)`, `geoRegion(world)`, `geoLonLat(world, x, z) → [lon, lat]`, `geoTile(world, lon, lat) → {x, z} | null` (null outside the window) and `geoKmPerTile(world, x?, z?)`. Other modules use only these.
- Land that runs off a regional window fades out rather than ending in a cliff (round 15): over its last 10 to 26 tiles (longer under high ground) its relief eases down to a low coastal plain, whose coast wanders about 5 to 25 tiles in from the border with capes and bays at two scales, then a shallow shelf deepens into a thin ring of deep sea at the border, with no beach (the terrain skirt and the water beyond the map continue the edge tiles outward and expect deep open sea there); the sea deepens toward the border so no shelf ends in a straight line. (The whole Earth keeps its deep-sea ring.)
- `world.meta.budgetArea` on regional maps is habitable land / 0.3, capped at 440k (the Vast whole Earth is about 345k), so a map of mostly land does not run several times Vast's population.
- Regional coastlines come from Natural Earth 1:10m (public domain): the union of the 1:10m admin-0 countries (npm `which-country-ne-10m`), with the larger 1:50m lakes (npm `sane-topojson`), rasterised at twice the tile resolution by `tools/region-masks.mjs` into `src/world/data/mask-<id>.js` (11–39 KB each). (`@geo-maps/earth-lands-10m` turned out to be OpenStreetMap data under the ODbL, so it was not used.) The game never ships the source data.
- Relief and climate reuse the World Map pipeline (`earthFeatures.js` is authored in longitude and latitude), scaled for the regional zoom so that ranges read as mountains, not cliffs or bumps (heights grow as tiles get finer: x1.2 on Europe, x1.65 on Japan, x1.9 on Taiwan; the great ranges have broad bodies of peaks, parallel ridges and valleys under their high sub-ranges), with extra regional detail where the whole-Earth data is too coarse (for example Taiwan's Central Mountain Range and western plain; the Japanese Alps; the Alps, Pyrenees, Carpathians and Scandinavian mountains; the Rockies, Sierra Nevada, Appalachians and Sierra Madre; the Himalaya, a rolling Tibetan plateau, Tian Shan and Altai).
- Lakes and rivers at regional scale: the major ones only (lakes from the mask plus a few the 1:50m data lacks, such as Biwa; rivers from earthFeatures.js plus regional ones in `src/world/data/regionFeatures.js`, which also holds the regional relief detail). The water surface is one sheet at sea level, so on every World Map world a lake holds water only in low country (a large upland lake such as Victoria sits in a broad shallow bowl); lakes on high ground (Tibet, the Altiplano, Issyk-Kul) are flat marshes, and no water is left at the bottom of a pit. River channels widen downstream and with finer tiles; regional rivers emerge where the ground falls below about 1.2 km, except the Hengduan rivers (Irrawaddy, Salween, Mekong, Yangtze) and the Brahmaputra's Great Bend, which cut parallel gorges from the plateau's edge.
- Snow and tree lines come from real elevation (each relief layer's units per km), latitude, distance from the ocean and moisture: no trees above the tree line (alpine grass, rock toward the snow), snow-capped Alps, Rockies, Caucasus, Himalaya and Fuji, rock and alpine grass on Taiwan's highest crests.
- The camera, minimap and Overview fit any aspect, including portrait maps; the World Map is never rotated (a portrait minimap keeps the panel's height rather than growing taller).
- Islands that carry realms stay land at regional sizes: on the Taiwan map Penghu (Magong), Green Island and Orchid Island are land, and Hualien and Penghu seat their realms; well-known cities checked on every regional map land on land or within one tile of it (Mumbai lies just west of Great East Asia's window).
- On the whole Earth: the Himalaya, Karakoram and Kunlun are `peaks` ranges (separate summits over deep saddles, and their capped front is scored by spurs); the Tibetan plateau rolls with its own ranges and has no terraced benches; narrow islands keep green shores; and the sea and its cold tint fade gradually toward the border so the map's top and bottom show no box edge.

## 3. Real realms and villages (owner: Civ — `src/sim/civ/*`, data in `src/sim/civ/places/*`)

- One data module per region id (`world`, `great-east-asia`, `asia-mainland`, `japan`, `taiwan`, `europe`, `north-america`): a list of realms `{ name, capital: { name, lon, lat }, cities: [{ name, lon, lat }], color?, culture? }`, using common English names and spellings (Taiwan place names in the forms used in Taiwan: Taipei, Keelung, Hsinchu, Taichung, Kaohsiung, …).
- **China is split** on the world, Great East Asia and Asia Mainland maps: **"Mainland Region"** holds the north (roughly north of the Qinling–Huai line, with its capital at Beijing) and **"ROC"** holds the south together with Taiwan (capital Nanjing; Taipei, Shanghai, Guangzhou and others are among its cities). Both are editable in game like any realm.
- **Japan map:** realms are Japan's traditional regions (Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Chubu, Kansai, Chugoku, Shikoku, Kyushu), plus the Korean realm whose coast lies inside the window. **Taiwan map:** realms are major cities and counties (for example Taipei, Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Taichung, Changhua, Chiayi, Tainan, Kaohsiung, Pingtung, Yilan, Hualien, Taitung, Penghu), each with real towns as villages.
- **Seeding:** on a World Map world, Civ places these realms instead of the random starts. Each capital becomes a village on the nearest suitable land tile within a small radius of its real position (via `geoTile`). A realm whose capital cannot be placed (outside the window, or no land nearby at this size) is skipped. Each realm gets a few of its largest cities as extra starting villages, within the village budget, leaving room for growth.
- **Growth keeps real names:** a new village founded inside a preloaded realm takes the nearest unused real city name of that realm (within a sensible distance), then a real name further out (a neighbouring realm's town), and only then a name in the style of the land (Japanese, Korean, pinyin, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay, Russian or Hindustani forms). An East Asian map never falls back to an English-sounding name.
- Realm names are shown as given (e.g. "Japan", "Mainland Region", "ROC", "Kanto"), without "Kingdom of". Colours stay distinct from neighbours; banners use the game's own emblems.
- **Realms that rise later** (rebels, splinter colonies, wanderers, a town freed by a god) also take plain real names, never "Kingdom of" or "Duchy of": the fallen real country the town belonged to ("France rises again", in its old colours when they are free), else the real country or region whose land the seat lies in when no living realm carries that name (Austria breaking away from Germany around Vienna), else the seat city's own name ("Kumamoto").
- **Ruler titles** on World Map worlds are real or neutral, never a culture's (no Jarl in Kyoto): a country's own where one reads naturally (Emperor of Japan, Tsar of Russia, Shah, Sultan, Khan, Maharaja, King or Queen of the monarchies), a daimyo on the Japan map, a magistrate on the Taiwan map, else Lord or Lady.
- **Real countries last:** the countries seeded at the start keep the map's premise for a century while wars, conquests and risings go on (see the notes below).
- Square presets keep their generated names, cultures, titles and random starts.

### Implementation notes (Civ, `src/sim/civ/realms.js`)

- Realms are listed most important first; `country(id, opts)` in `places/countries.js` builds a region's copy of a shared country (`first` reorders its cities for that map, `edge: true` seats a realm whose capital lies outside the window at its first city inside it, e.g. Russia at Vladivostok on Great East Asia or Korea at Busan on Japan). A capital on rugged high ground gets a levelled pad for its hall, and a realm whose capital inside the window still finds no site is seated at the first of its next three cities that does (round 25: Australia, Mexico and Iran on the whole Earth and Mexico and Central America on the North America map were never seated before); a realm with cities that must exist keeps its capital. `color` is matched to the nearest heraldic tincture unless a neighbour wears one too close; `culture` overrides the culture read from the land.
- Counts: up to 20 realms and 26 starting villages on Vast and the regional maps (never more than three quarters of the village budget), scaled by the square root of the tile count on smaller maps (Large: 10 realms, 13 villages; Huge: 15 and 20). An explicit `?civs=` caps both. Extra cities go to the bigger realms first (the first two realms share alike).
- Cities that must exist at the start (section 4; `MUST` in `places/countries.js`, asked for with `country(id, { must: true })`): their realms are seated whatever their rank (North and South Korea on every whole-Earth size) without taking a usual realm's place; the cities are laid out right after the capitals, at their real places when the usual spacing allows, else at the nearest site with the spacing eased step by step (down to about 3 tiles on Large), and an island a tile wide at this size (Puerto Rico) or a coast already full gets a 3x3 pad of low shore. The usual starting cities that follow ignore them when spacing, so none of them is dropped (the census on seed 7 shows the usual set unchanged on Large, Huge, Vast, Great East Asia and North America). `country(id, { capital })` reseats a realm (the ROC at Taipei); its old seat stays among its starting towns.
- Peoples (`src/sim/civ/peoples.js`): every real realm has a people (`PEOPLE` in `countries.js`; `people` on the Japan and Taiwan regions) and its culture id is `${style}~${people}` (for example `vale~japanese`): buildings, dress and costs read the style before the `~`, personal names come from the people's pool (pinyin for the Mainland Region, Taiwan romanization for the ROC and the Taiwan map, and pools for Japanese, Korean, the languages of Europe, the Americas, Asia and Africa), and the realm card reads "Chinese realm". A people that grows from one later keeps it (`vale~japanese~3`, "Sapporo Japanese"). Square maps keep the generated cultures and names.
- Spacing at the start: 9 tiles within a realm, 10 between realms on one landmass, 7 across water. A city whose real position already has a neighbour that close is skipped rather than pushed elsewhere. A capital may move up to 6 tiles on the whole Earth (more on the regional maps, up to 16) to find ground; on rugged or narrow land (Taiwan or Japan on the whole Earth) a small pad is levelled.
- Naming: a village founded later takes the nearest real name not held by a living village: its own realm's cities within about 4.5 degrees (9 to 20 tiles), else any listed place within about 3.5 degrees (7 to 16 tiles); failing both the search widens to twice and then three times those distances, and then falls back to the land's naming style (`lang` on the realm data: `ja`, `ko`, `zh`, `tw`, `vi`, `th`, `ms`, `ru`, `hi`; East Asian maps default to theirs; Europe and the Americas use `en`, `fr`, `de`, `es`, `it`, `pt`), and only where there is none to the culture's generated name. Settlers of an independent colony still look among their parent realm's cities. Conquered villages keep their names; a razed city's name can be taken again. The Japan and Taiwan lists carry about 540 and 350 places (towns in Japanese romanization; Taiwan in the spellings used there).
- A realm entry with `seed: false` is never seeded; its places only name land at the window's edge (on the Japan map North Korea's east coast, the Russian Far East and the Chinese border country; on the Taiwan map the Fujian coast). `title: {m, f}` gives a realm's ruler title (`null`: neutral), `REGION_STYLE` in `places/index.js` the map's own title and naming style.
- Real realms carry `plain: true` (notes and labels say "Japan", never "the Japan") and `realm` (their index in the region's list); realms seeded at the start also carry `seeded: true`. A realm that rises later and takes a real country's name gets that `realm` index too; one named after its seat is `plain` without it.
- Unions on World Map worlds: a real country is never folded into a generated realm (when the two unite the real one stays, whatever their sizes); a seeded country keeps its own crown for 70 years and then joins only a firm ally (opinion 40+) of three or more towns while it has one; any other real country keeps its crown for 30 years. A generated realm of up to two towns (three on the regional maps) at least 8 years old joins a real neighbour it is allied with, rejoins the country it broke from (or the real country whose land it stands in) after 8 years of peace (6 on the regional maps), or joins a bordering real country after 12 years of peace (10 on the regional maps).
- Resilience of real countries: the first real country to hold a town is its `home`; a town held by another realm while its home stands has its loyalty lowered by 8 and, when it rises, goes back to its home (nearby towns of the same home may follow) instead of crowning a realm of its own, and a capital won back is the seat again. A real country's own towns gain 5 loyalty (3 for one that rose later). A seeded country rides out one rising every 4 years, a contested succession splits it less often, and in its first 40 years wars against it are declared a little less often; a seeded country of one town is never fought by two realms at once. When the last town of a real country falls in war, its court flees with the townsfolk who escape to one of the country's own free real cities nearby and the conqueror makes a 12-year peace (once in 15 years); otherwise the country falls, and may rise again when one of its towns rebels. A real country's settlers strike out as a realm of their own a third as often as elsewhere (other settlers on World Map worlds about half as often).
- Migrants (round 35): every move of people between towns on a World Map world (key-city refills by land or sea, families sent to a dwindling realm or a hamlet, merging hamlets, refugees, wanderers seeking a town, a fleeing court) goes no further than 60 tiles to a town of another realm and 80 tiles within the realm; a key city with no town in reach is kept by newcomers from beyond (up to 8 people).

## 4. Round 14 changes (user requests)

### Regional maps hold only their own lands (owner: Geography)

- **Japan map:** only Japanese land. The Korean peninsula, the Russian Far East (including Sakhalin) and any Chinese land inside the window become open sea, with the usual deep-sea treatment at the border. No Korean or Russian realm is seeded there, and no `seed: false` edge places are needed for that land.
- **Taiwan map:** only land under ROC rule today: Taiwan, Penghu, Green Island, Orchid Island, **Kinmen** and **Matsu** and the smaller islets around them. The window is **widened** to take in Kinmen (about 118.2–118.5°E, 24.4–24.5°N) and Matsu (about 119.9–120.5°E, 25.9–26.4°N), roughly 118.0–122.3°E and 21.6–26.5°N, keeping a portrait, Vast-class map whose dimensions suit the chunking. The Fujian coast inside the window becomes open sea. Kinmen County (seat Jincheng) and Lienchiang County (Matsu, seat Nangan) are added to the Taiwan map's realms with their towns.
- **Asia Mainland map:** **narrowed to the East Asian mainland**: China (both the Mainland Region and the ROC's mainland lands, including Tibet and Xinjiang), Mongolia and the Korean peninsula; window roughly 73–135°E and 17.5–54°N, Vast-class, dimensions chosen as in section 1 (about 832–896 × 640). Every island becomes open sea (Hainan, Taiwan, the Japanese islands, Sakhalin and the rest). Land of other countries that falls inside the rectangle (the Russian Far East and Siberia, Kazakhstan and Central Asia, the Himalayan south slope, northern Myanmar, Laos and Vietnam) stays as unclaimed wilderness at the edges: no realm of those countries is seeded and they name no places. Only East Asian realms are seeded: Mainland Region, ROC (mainland cities, capital Nanjing), Mongolia, North Korea and South Korea. The display name stays "Asia Mainland".
- Coastlines elsewhere stay as they are; the masks are regenerated by `tools/region-masks.mjs` with the excluded land removed.

### ROC capital and additional starting cities (owner: Civ)

- On the **whole-Earth World Map** (Large, Huge, Vast) and the **Great East Asia** map the ROC's capital (seat) is **Taipei**, as its temporary capital. On the Asia Mainland map (no Taiwan) the ROC keeps Nanjing.
- On the whole-Earth World Map, the Great East Asia map and the North America map, each of these cities **exists at the start** as a village of the realm named, wherever it lies inside the map's window. They come in addition to the current starting villages (raise the starting cap as needed; they never replace an existing starting city):

| Realm | Cities that must exist at the start |
|---|---|
| Mainland Region | Shenyang, Harbin, Urumqi, Lhasa, Chongqing, Nanning |
| North Korea | Pyongyang, Hamhung |
| South Korea | Seoul |
| Japan | Sapporo, Kumamoto |
| United States | Anchorage, Seattle, San Diego, Houston, Chicago, Denver, San Juan (Puerto Rico) |
| ROC | Hong Kong, Haikou, Fuzhou |

- Ownership follows this table even where the north–south split would say otherwise: Chongqing and Nanning belong to the Mainland Region; Puerto Rico (San Juan) belongs to the United States, not a realm of its own.
- North Korea and South Korea are separate realms on these maps.
- On smaller whole-Earth sizes (Large) a listed city may sit closer to another than the usual spacing; it is still placed (it may be nudged up to a few tiles onto land), never skipped.
